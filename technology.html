NEURALINK · THE HARDWARE AND THE SIGNAL PATH

Four components.
One connection.

The implant records neural activity, processes the signals and sends them to an external application. Start with the four components in Neuralink’s own technology overview.

What is inside the implant?

01 · ENCLOSURE

A protected interior

A biocompatible, hermetically sealed housing protects the implant electronics in the body’s environment.

02 · BATTERY

Wireless charging

A small battery powers the device. An external inductive charger replenishes it without a cable passing through the skin.

03 · ELECTRONICS

Processing and transmission

Custom low-power electronics process neural signals and transmit them wirelessly to the Neuralink Application on an external device.

04 · THREADS

A local neural interface

Very thin, flexible electrode threads record neural activity in cortex. A thread is not a wire collecting signals from every part of the head.

Paraphrased from Neuralink’s technology overview. Our exploded model is an original educational schematic, not an engineering replica or implantation guide.

Where are the threads implanted?

Electrode placement is task-specific. This website does not assign Neuralink threads to the coordinates of a different research system. The exploded device view explains its parts without presenting an unverified brain implantation map.

Which machine does what?

The surgical robot places electrode threads during implantation. The external computer receives transmitted data and runs decoding software. These are separate roles. In our research replay, the laptop is a generic illustration of the external-device role, and the small network on its screen represents software.

What does our speech example demonstrate?

A separately labelled research replay shows one possible signal-to-text workflow using public recordings. It illustrates an idea; it does not measure Neuralink’s performance. Its optional voice is standard browser text-to-speech.

Research note: the example decoder and recording sites

The recorded example comes from Willett et al. (2023), not Neuralink. A recurrent neural network predicted speech-sound units, and a language-model decoder generated likely words. The study recorded two arrays in area 6v and two in area 44; subsequent speech-decoding analyses used 6v recordings.

Those array positions belong to that study. They are not a map of Neuralink thread locations. The separate replay uses approximate region markers on a generic head, not the participant’s MRI.
